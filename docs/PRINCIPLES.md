# Editorial and Product Principles

These principles define what the directory should protect as it grows.

## 1. Help churches make better decisions

The purpose is not to maximise clicks to suppliers. It is to help a church choose an appropriate tool, or recognise that it does not need one.

## 2. Trust is the core asset

The site's defensibility will come from structured, current, UK-relevant and honestly qualified information.

A polished page containing guessed information is worse than an incomplete page that names what remains unknown.

## 3. Independence comes before monetisation

Commercial relationships may fund the work later, but they must not determine rankings, remove legitimate criticism or create hidden endorsements.

Every affiliate or sponsored relationship must be visible.

## 4. Do not manufacture urgency

Do not use countdowns, false scarcity, exaggerated savings or fear-based claims.

Church software decisions often affect sensitive personal data, money, safeguarding and long-term workflows. They deserve deliberation.

## 5. Recommend simplicity when simplicity is enough

A small church may be better served by a spreadsheet, shared calendar, established finance package or simpler process.

The site should be willing to say so.

## 6. Separate fact, supplier claim and assessment

Readers should be able to tell:

- what the supplier states
- what an official document confirms
- what the directory concludes
- what has not been established

## 7. UK relevance must be demonstrated

Do not describe a product as UK-friendly merely because UK customers can access it.

Consider Gift Aid, pounds sterling, UK payment systems, data protection, charity structures, safeguarding administration, support hours and local terminology where relevant.

## 8. Depth is more valuable than catalogue size

Twenty excellent listings are more useful than three hundred shallow entries.

Expansion should not outrun the ability to research, review and maintain the content.

## 9. Technology cannot solve every organisational problem

Software does not replace healthy leadership, clear responsibility, good safeguarding culture, pastoral judgement or sound financial controls.

Guides should name the limits of technology.

## 10. Corrections should be easy

Suppliers and users should be able to report inaccurate or outdated information. Corrections should be assessed against evidence rather than accepted or rejected according to commercial status.

## 11. Rankings require transparent criteria

Do not create a `best` label or overall score without a visible and defensible method.

Different churches have different needs. The site should often explain fit and trade-offs rather than produce one universal winner.

## 12. Privacy-conscious measurement

Collect only the analytics needed to understand whether the site is useful. Avoid intrusive tracking and unnecessary personal data.

## 13. AI assists, humans remain accountable

AI may help structure information, identify missing fields, draft summaries and maintain consistency.

AI must not be treated as a reliable source of current product facts. Published claims require evidence and human review.

## 14. Build a decision resource, not only a directory

The long-term product should help users move from:

```text
We think we need new software
```

to:

```text
We understand the problem, our requirements, the trade-offs, the suitable options and the next responsible step.
```
