# Growth Strategy

## Purpose and thesis

This is the persistent growth and promotion strategy for
[churchsoftware.co.uk](https://churchsoftware.co.uk). It is a planning and
execution reference, not authority to send outreach, publish content or change
the site without a separately scoped task and the normal evidence review.

The commercial purpose is to test whether a useful, independent UK church
software decision resource can earn sustained demand and, only later, support
transparent commercial relationships. The editorial purpose is more immediate:
help churches make safer, better-informed choices about systems that affect
people, money, safeguarding, data and long-lived working practices.

Early success means evidence of *relevant decision use*: a church administrator
uses a guide or checklist, a visitor makes a comparison, a supplier corrects a
fact with evidence, or an established church organisation shares a genuinely
useful resource. A small number of these signals is more valuable than a large
number of casual page views. Traffic quality matters because the site is for
people making consequential, often infrequent purchasing decisions—not a
general-interest audience.

The central thesis is:

> Do not merely generate more content. Create genuinely useful decision
> resources, evidence-backed datasets and outreach assets, then use existing
> organisations, suppliers and communities to distribute them. Social content
> distributes the product; it is not the product.

Generic AI articles and mass social posting are weak strategies: they are easy
to reproduce, difficult to verify, rarely earn durable trust and can consume a
maintainer's time without improving a church's decision. Defensible value comes
from accurate UK-specific product information, comparison and decision pages,
original structured data, trusted editorial judgement, supplier corrections,
practical tools, existing networks sharing useful resources, systematic
internal links and continual improvement using actual search data. The aim is a
trusted decision resource, not merely a long software list or an affiliate
funnel.

## Supplier correction and distribution campaign

### Objective and selection

Use source-backed corrections to improve factual accuracy first and create a legitimate
reason for suppliers to notice and, if they choose, share a corrected listing.
It is not a request for a favourable review, a reciprocal link campaign or a
sales programme.

Contact first where a listing is already public or planned, the product has
clear UK relevance, a correction could materially improve a buyer's decision,
and there is a credible official route to evidence. Prioritise products with
high search interest or comparison relevance, materially stale/high-value
fields, an active UK customer base indicated by evidence, and suppliers whose
audience overlaps the site's target users. Deprioritise unsupported products,
poor-fit international products and suppliers with no identifiable business
contact route.

### Pre-contact listing audit

Before contact, an agent prepares an evidence pack; Bryn reviews it before an
email is sent.

1. Check the official site, recorded sources and each source's checked date.
2. Mark every claim as confirmed, supplier claim, independently evidenced, not
   confirmed or possibly outdated, using the site's evidence model.
3. Identify the exact fields that would affect a UK church's decision: UK
   purchasing availability; GBP price, VAT and contract terms; free plan/trial;
   Gift Aid and UK payment workflow; data-processing agreement, hosting and
   transfers; export, migration, permissions/audit trail and support.
4. Separate factual corrections from the directory's independent summary,
   limitations, methodology and any editorial assessment.
5. Record the official URLs, access date, a concise proposed wording and the
   specific evidence requested. Do not quote or copy substantial supplier copy.

### Contact, evidence and update workflow

Find a role-based public route first: press, partnerships, customer success,
marketing, product, legal/privacy or a published general business contact.
Avoid guessing private addresses or researching individuals beyond publicly
provided professional contact details. The initial email should be short and
contain: the directory's independent purpose; the listing URL; a factual,
non-promotional summary of what is being checked; a small numbered list of
fields/questions; a request for public URLs or written confirmation from an
authorised representative; the correction process; and the statement that
accuracy does not give editorial control.

Ask for corrections, evidence and clarifications—not approval. A supplier may
correct a factual error with evidence, but cannot require removal of fair,
evidence-backed limitations or direct the listing's conclusion. On receipt,
log the reply, check whether it supports the claimed field, retain source
provenance, update only supported fields, preserve uncertainty where it
remains, and ask Bryn to approve contested or consequential changes. State the
listing's updated date and source provenance honestly.

Once a factual update is live, send a concise note: thank the supplier, link to
the accurate listing, restate independence, and invite—not pressure—them to
share it with customers or followers if they believe it is useful. Do not ask
for a backlink; a share should have a clear value to their audience, such as a
neutral UK comparison, buying checklist or source-backed product record.

Send one polite follow-up after 7–14 working days, then a final close-out after
another 14–21 working days only if the facts are decision-critical. Stop after
two follow-ups, an explicit decline, a request not to be contacted, bounced
mail without an alternative public route, or any hostile/disproportionate
interaction. Maintain the existing evidence rather than implying a response.

| Status | Meaning and permitted next action |
| --- | --- |

| Contacted | Bryn has sent the approved email; log date and channel. |
| Awaiting reply | No action before the planned follow-up date. |

| Listing updated | Approved factual update is published with provenance. |

Use a tracker with product, listing URL, contact route, requested fields,
sources, status, dates, reply summary, evidence links, proposed change,
editorial decision, owner and next action. Keep it private unless consent and a
public purpose justify disclosure.

### Boundaries and responsibilities

An agent can locate public contacts, audit public evidence, prepare a tracker,
draft emails and change proposals, detect staleness and summarise replies.
Bryn must approve emails, send relationship-based outreach, approve material
corrections, decide disputed claims and handle any commercial discussion.

Supplier correction is not endorsement: use visible wording such as “facts updated
with supplier evidence” rather than “approved” or “partner”. If a supplier asks
to remove criticism, distinguish an erroneous factual statement (correct it
when evidenced) from a fair, attributed limitation or uncertainty (retain it,
or revise for clarity after editorial review). Escalate legal threats or
substantive disputes to Bryn; do not argue automatically. Affiliate, sponsored
listing, advertising or preferential-placement proposals must be handled in a
separate commercial thread, with explicit disclosure and no promise of positive
coverage, rank or outcome.

## Original UK church software dataset

The dataset is the strongest long-term asset when it remains source-backed,
current and clearly qualified. It should extend the existing content schema and
evidence model only through separately approved implementation work; this
strategy does not authorise schema changes.

### Proposed fields

Record, where evidenced and relevant: product/categories; operational or church
size indicators; pricing model and source-backed UK pricing; free plan/trial;
Gift Aid functions; UK payment and GBP support; charity discounts; UK office or
support hours; data-hosting statements; data-processing agreement; export and
migration assistance; safeguarding administration; children's check-in;
volunteer rotas; multi-site support; accounting, website and mobile-app
integrations; accessibility information; contract/cancellation requirements;
onboarding; evidence sources; last checked date; and field-level provenance.

The highest editorial and commercial value fields are UK purchasing availability,
total-cost questions (including VAT, implementation and transaction costs),
Gift Aid/payment workflow, data protection/hosting/transfers, export and exit,
migration, support, permissions and audit capabilities, and practical
operational fit. They answer costly questions that generic supplier directories
often leave vague. Hosting location, UK GDPR/legal claims, accessibility,
safeguarding capability, integrations, contract terms and “church size served”
are especially risky or difficult: retain the supplier's precise statement and
source, do not infer a conclusion, omit incidental gaps and turn material gaps
into supplier questions.

### Maintenance and provenance

Every public field needs a supporting source URL, source type, retrieved or
checked date, evidence state and optional concise note explaining scope. Flag a
field for review when its source is older than the configured threshold, returns
a confirmed failure, has a changed page title/content signal, conflicts with a
newer official source, or a supplier submits credible evidence. Supplier
corrections update the same provenance record; it does not erase the prior
editorial decision or create an overall completeness badge.

The agent can suggest stale records and structured changes, but a human must
confirm scope and publish. Do not copy protected supplier tables or marketing
copy: extract only short factual propositions, attribute their official source
and write independent summaries. Internal notes may include research queries,
unpublished change history, contact/log details and ambiguity analysis. The
public dataset must contain only publishable facts, qualified evidence metadata
and links—never private correspondence, personal data or speculative notes.

The dataset can responsibly produce product comparisons, capability landing
pages, supplier-question lists, change summaries, category reports and factual
social insights. It must never generate a hidden quality score or treat blanks
as “No”.

### Periodic report: *The State of UK Church Software*

Publish only after the dataset has sufficient coverage and an explicit method.
The report can state scope, dates, inclusion rules, source mix and data
limits; describe observed pricing-publication patterns, field completeness,
common buyer questions or documented capability availability; and include
charts such as category coverage, evidence-state distribution, pricing-model
availability, date freshness and anonymised question themes. Publication assets
could include the report, methodology, chart images, a data dictionary, a
downloadable summary and a media/partner briefing. Do not pre-state findings,
invent percentages or imply market-wide representation beyond the sample.

## Social content as distribution

Social content normally begins with a stronger asset: a comparison, dataset
finding, decision tool or well-evidenced guide. For each substantial asset,
prepare one longer LinkedIn post, three short LinkedIn posts, one carousel
outline, one poll, one Facebook-group discussion prompt, one newsletter item,
several factual quote/insight-card concepts, one follow-up responding to real
replies, and—when factually appropriate—one supplier-specific post. These are
drafting outputs, not permission to post.

Useful content is specific, evidence-backed, relevant to real church
administration, frank about trade-offs, not generic productivity advice, not
disguised advertising, and able to invite discussion without manufactured
controversy. Possible angles include the questions a treasurer should ask about
total cost; what “data export” needs to mean before a migration; the difference
between an evidenced fact and a supplier question; or the decision a small
church can simplify before buying anything. Do not draft final posts here or
claim results not supported by the asset.

Bryn's personal LinkedIn profile is likely to have more early distribution value
than a new branded account because it carries an existing human identity,
context and trust. Use it carefully: personal experience must be clearly
distinguished from site research, and community rules and relationships still
govern what is posted.

## Internal linking system

Use contextual links to help readers continue a decision, not to manipulate
anchors. Software pages may link to categories, direct comparisons, alternatives,
church-size/use-case guides, relevant capability guides, migration guidance,
related products and appropriate external evidence. Category pages link to
products, buying guides, tools, related categories and selected comparisons.
Guides link to relevant listings, category pages, comparison pages, tools and
related guides. Comparisons link to every compared profile, relevant categories,
alternatives, methodology and buyer guides.

Run a build-time or local audit after substantial content changes. It should
report orphan pages, broken links, generic/weak anchors, too few incoming
internal links, excessive repeated exact-match anchors, missing contextual
links, broken comparison URLs, category pages without supporting guides and new
pages not linked from a hub or index. Treat reports as proposals: the agent
should identify the reader's next likely question, suggest the most relevant
target and natural anchor, explain why it fits the surrounding sentence, and
omit a suggestion where no honest contextual link exists. Do not auto-insert
links merely to satisfy a count.

## Search data feedback loop

Once configured with consent-appropriate tools, use Google Search Console,
Bing Webmaster Tools, Cloudflare Web Analytics and outbound-product click
tracking where available. Every fortnight initially, then monthly when volume
is low, an agent should export/inspect the period against the previous one and
produce a ranked report.

Check queries with impressions but few clicks; pages roughly positions 5–20;
poor click-through rates; unexpected queries; missing comparisons; weak titles
or descriptions; traffic without meaningful engagement; outbound clicks; zero-
traffic pages; and crawl/indexing problems. Compare Search Console with Bing,
indexing/sitemap data and on-site behaviour before making conclusions. Low data
is an observation, not proof that a topic has failed.

| Finding | Recommended action | Expected impact | Effort | Evidence | Human judgement? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Specific query/page] | [Concrete tested change] | High/medium/low | S/M/L | [Dates, tool and data] | Yes/No and why |

The recurring output must include findings, recommendation, expected impact,
implementation effort, evidence, priority and whether human judgement is
needed. Bryn approves interpretive changes. Real search data should
progressively replace speculative keyword planning.

## What not to prioritise

- Hundreds of shallow AI-generated listings: they multiply evidence-review debt and
  dilute the trust asset.
- Generic daily social posts: without a valuable underlying asset they create
  noise, not durable demand.
- Mass automated outreach: it damages relationships and cannot represent a
  nuanced independent editorial position.
- Buying backlinks: it risks search penalties and conflicts with credible
  editorial growth.
- Fake user reviews or software rankings: both are deceptive; rankings need a
  visible, defensible method.
- Unsupported product data: a plausible error about cost, data or safeguarding
  is more damaging than an incomplete record.
- Aggressive affiliate design: it creates implied endorsement and weakens
  decision confidence.
- A newsletter before recurring value and an audience exist: it adds operational
  work without a clear reason to subscribe.
- Every social platform: spread attention only where a defined audience and
  value exchange exist.
- User reviews before sustainable moderation: sensitive, defamatory or
  misleading claims need responsible review.
- Paid advertising before conversion paths are understood: it buys noise before
  the site knows what useful action looks like.
- Repeated minor keyword pages: they fragment authority and create thin,
  duplicative content.
- Vanity traffic unrelated to church software decisions: it makes the wrong
  work appear successful.

## Metrics

Use a compact dashboard and annotate material changes. Leading indicators:
indexed pages, organic impressions, relevant ranking queries, directory
searches, filter use, comparisons created, product outbound clicks, supplier
responses, correction submissions and partner outreach progress. Useful
outcomes: organic clicks, click-through rate, supplier shares, referring
domains, partner placements, repeat visitors, decision-tool use, and email
subscribers when a newsletter is relevant. Track maintenance time per listing
to protect sustainability; track revenue only after monetisation begins.

Vanity metrics include unqualified page views, followers, impressions without
relevant clicks, likes without discussion or referral traffic unrelated to a
software decision. They may provide context but should not select the roadmap.

## 90-day post-launch growth plan

### Phase A — clarify and instrument (days 1–30)

Complete the 30-day plan. The primary output is a simpler homepage and a
measurable route into the existing directory, shortlist and comparison tools.
Do not use the month to add a large listing batch.

### Phase B — compress and connect (days 31–60)

Ship the validated compact browse and product-summary layer, then replace the
comparison checkbox wall with searchable selection. Preserve static HTML, URL
state, field-level evidence and keyboard operation. Use comparison combinations,
directory searches and Search Console queries to select at most one new
high-intent comparison or alternatives brief.

### Phase C — acquire and learn (days 61–90)

Publish only the approved free-and-affordable anchor and no more than two
supporting pages. Prepare a small supplier-correction batch and distribution
pack around the strongest completed asset. Review acquisition, decision-tool
use, evidence freshness and maintenance effort before expanding the cluster or
starting supplier-commercial work.

## Reusable campaign workflow

For every substantial page, tool, dataset or report:

1. Identify the audience and concrete decision problem.
2. Research search intent and evidence; define scope, exclusions and source
   requirements.
3. Create the asset with an explicit useful outcome.
4. Perform factual, editorial, accessibility and independence review.
5. Add only relevant internal links and hub/index links.
6. Create accurate metadata and visible-content-compatible structured data.
7. Prepare a distribution pack derived from the asset.
8. Identify suppliers and organisations with a genuine audience reason to care.
9. Prepare personalised outreach for Bryn's approval; never send automatically.
10. Publish only through an approved implementation/release task.
11. Distribute through the selected, appropriate channels.
12. Monitor search, referral, engagement and correction signals.
13. Update based on evidence, preserving provenance and an editorial record.
14. Record lessons, effort, results, next review date and whether to expand,
    revise, pause or stop.

## Operating rules for future agents

- Do not confuse content volume with progress.
- Do not publish product facts without evidence.
- Do not send outreach automatically.
- Do not promise suppliers positive coverage.
- Do not create commercial relationships without disclosure.
- Do not build new channels without a defined audience and value exchange.
- Prefer assets that can earn search traffic, links and direct sharing.
- Use real performance data to decide what to expand.
- Record sources, decisions and results.
- Protect trust even when doing so reduces short-term traffic.
