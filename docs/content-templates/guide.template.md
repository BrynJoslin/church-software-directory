Answer the main question, identify the intended UK church context and state the
most important qualification. Record the research date or point to the dated
source section. Keep the complete opening under 150 words.

## Quick answer

Give the answer a time-poor reader needs. Use a short list or comparison table
when there is more than one legitimate route. Do not declare a universal winner
without a published method and supporting evidence.

## Who this guide is for and how it was researched

Define the audience, decision boundary, exclusions, product-inclusion criteria,
market-coverage limit, research method, testing status and any relevant
commercial relationship.

## When software may not be the answer

Explain the operational problem and when an existing tool, spreadsheet, shared
calendar, documented process or specialist product may be sufficient. Name the
organisational problem software cannot resolve.

## What to compare

Explain the topic-specific criteria that would genuinely change a shortlist.
Cover relevant UK, cost, data, accessibility, implementation, integration and
exit questions. Do not reproduce a generic feature list.

## How the options differ

Apply the same criteria to every option. For each, explain why it is in scope,
the context it may fit, its evidence-backed differentiator, a limitation or
unknown and the first point to verify.

## Software listings to explore

Link to at least two relevant software listings already published on this site.
Explain why each relates to the decision and state that the links are not a
ranking or endorsement. Keep the guide complete without a supplier click.

## A practical decision tool

Provide a usable comparison table, requirements checklist, total-cost method,
trial script, supplier-question list, migration checklist or decision-record
outline. It must be specific enough for a church team to use.

## Implementation, ownership and exit

Explain who should own the process, what to test before commitment, likely
training or migration work, review points and how the church can retrieve its
information or leave.

## Your next step

Give one concrete, proportionate next action.

## Sources and research limits

State the research date, market-coverage limit, important unknowns, testing
status and advice boundary. List the most important sources with explicit
accessed dates. Use current supplier sources for product facts and authoritative
UK sources for regulatory, tax, security and governance context.
