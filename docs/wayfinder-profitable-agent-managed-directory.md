# Wayfinder: Profitable, agent-managed Church Software UK

**Status:** active
**Last updated:** 31 July 2026
**Canonical location:** `docs/wayfinder-profitable-agent-managed-directory.md`

## Destination

Church Software UK is a trusted, useful decision resource for UK churches that
earns sustained operating profit while agents perform most routine research,
maintenance, analysis, implementation and growth preparation. Bryn's planned
involvement is small and concentrated in batched approvals, commercial
decisions and genuine exceptions.

The destination is not a promise that reputational harm can never occur. It is
an operating system that keeps risk within an agreed tolerance, detects
problems early, stops unsafe activity and preserves accountable human judgement
where the consequences could materially affect Bryn, churches or suppliers.

### Evidence of arrival

The following thresholds are agreed:

- **Meaningful profit:** at least £500 monthly operating profit for three
  consecutive months. The calculation includes attributable software, hosting,
  professional services, refunds, agent or contractor costs and Bryn's time at
  an agreed internal rate; tax is reported separately. Further growth belongs
  in a separate wayfinder once this target is reached.
- **Low owner input:** no more than two planned hours of Bryn's time per month.
- **Reputation control:** for six consecutive months there are no undisclosed
  commercial relationships, unauthorised public messages, fabricated claims or
  unresolved high-severity factual incidents.

The result must be experienced by:

- UK church decision-makers, who receive useful, current and honestly bounded
  information;
- suppliers and partners, who receive fair treatment and clear commercial
  boundaries;
- Bryn, whose name, relationships and time are protected by exception-based
  governance rather than constant supervision; and
- the agent system, which has precise authority, stop conditions and handoff
  rules.

### Boundaries and non-negotiables

- Trust and decision usefulness remain the core asset; revenue is not allowed
  to determine factual treatment, prominence, comparison outcomes or editorial
  conclusions.
- Agents may not invent product facts, experience, testimonials, rankings,
  compliance claims, commercial relationships or performance results.
- High-consequence public claims, criticism, recommendations, supplier
  disputes, relationship-based outreach, commercial terms and changes based on
  private correspondence retain accountable human approval.
- Agent autonomy is risk-tiered. Reversible private analysis and routine
  technical maintenance can be highly automated; external speech and material
  editorial or commercial judgement cannot be silently delegated.
- Commercial content must be obviously identifiable. The point-of-use
  disclosure standard is stricter than relying on a general disclosure page.
- Personal data, church-member data and sensitive correspondence are not used
  to make the operation appear more automated.
- Catalogue or content growth must not outrun evidence review and freshness
  capacity.
- The site remains accessible, statically generated and maintainable without a
  large framework, database or CMS unless evidence later establishes a real
  need.

## Current position

### Known facts

- The site is live at `https://churchsoftware.co.uk`.
- The live site showed 147 researched software profiles and 552 dated source
  links on 31 July 2026. The repository contains 17 categories and 27 guides.
- The directory already provides static search and filtering, two-to-four
  product comparisons, a guided shortlist, decision packs, methodology,
  correction routes, structured data exports and a charity-discount register.
- The content model distinguishes supplier sources, independent sources,
  directory testing and unresolved material questions. Listings have a
  180-day stale threshold.
- Consent-gated GA4 is configured. The repository does not contain the Search
  Console, Bing Webmaster Tools or GA4 performance baseline required to judge
  qualified demand.
- Three software records currently declare affiliate relationships: Logos
  Bible Study, Ticket Tailor and Hallmaster. No sponsored listing is currently
  published. Current revenue, clicks, conversions, commission terms and
  attributable operating costs are not recorded in the repository.
- The public disclosure says affiliate links are labelled at the point of use,
  do not influence editorial treatment and may earn commission. The site says
  suppliers cannot buy a paid ranking.
- The existing roadmap has implemented the acquisition surface, compact browse
  layer and improved comparison discovery. Visible freshness, supplier
  participation, commercial readiness and original market reporting remain
  later, gated work.
- The documented growth strategy already rejects mass AI content, mass
  automated outreach, bought links, fake reviews, unsupported rankings and
  automatic publication of consequential claims.
- UK official guidance makes clear that affiliate marketing must be obviously
  identifiable, commercial relationships on review-style sites should be
  disclosed clearly and prominently, and electronic outreach must be assessed
  under PECR and data-protection rules.
- The current branch is `codex/canonical-evidence-taxonomy`. It contains
  uncommitted work outside this map; that work has not been altered.

### Existing assets and commitments

- A coherent independent brand, UK-specific proposition and live domain.
- A large structured evidence base with public methodology and corrections.
- Strong static architecture, validation scripts, sitemap/link/accessibility
  checks and a local maintenance dashboard.
- Published buying guides, comparison paths and shortlist tooling that can
  serve high-intent visitors without creating new content volume.
- A persistent growth strategy, phased roadmap and explicit agent/human
  responsibility boundary.
- Existing affiliate capability and three disclosed affiliate records, which
  provide a small live commercial surface to measure before adding formats.
- A public supplier-update route and an evidence-led supplier correction
  workflow that can later support distribution and commercial readiness.
- A central implementation-status record and a culture of recording sources,
  limitations and deferred work.

### Constraints and risks

- There is not yet enough accessible performance or financial evidence to say
  the site has repeatable demand, earns revenue or has workable unit economics.
- Search and purchasing cycles in this niche are likely to be infrequent. A
  large catalogue can generate substantial maintenance work before it
  generates meaningful revenue.
- A nominally profitable site can still be uneconomic if Bryn's approval,
  relationship or incident-handling time is ignored.
- Full public autonomy conflicts with the project's existing principle that AI
  assists while humans remain accountable. Removing that control would create
  reputation risk rather than merely save time.
- Affiliate income can create perceived bias even when treatment is unchanged.
  Sponsorship, lead sale and referral models increase this risk and require
  stronger separation, disclosure and commercial governance.
- Supplier outreach or community posting can affect Bryn's relationships. It
  cannot safely be treated like an internal maintenance task.
- The project currently exposes a broad, recently researched catalogue. The
  ongoing 180-day review burden must be measured before more listings are
  added.
- “No negative impact on reputation” is not directly guaranteeable. The
  operable target is bounded risk, rapid correction, transparent disclosure,
  no unauthorised representation and evidence that trust indicators are not
  deteriorating.

### Assumptions to test

- Qualified organic demand is large enough to support at least one commercial
  model without broadening into general church technology media.
- The most reputation-compatible initial revenue mix is transparent affiliate
  income plus a church-paid, productised decision resource; supplier
  sponsorship and paid placement should remain deferred until measured demand
  and governance exist.
- Existing shortlist, comparison and guide assets can produce useful decision
  signals before another significant content expansion.
- Agents can reduce Bryn's effort to the recommended two-hour ceiling through
  risk scoring, evidence-linked change packs, batched approval and
  exception-only alerts.
- The current 147-profile catalogue can be maintained to the published standard
  within the economics of the site. If not, scope should contract rather than
  allow freshness or evidence quality to decay.
- A modest number of high-intent visitors can support profitability if the
  value per qualified conversion is high enough; large general traffic is not
  required.

## Frontier

### Ready now

- **Document attributable operating costs**
  - Type: research
  - Question or outcome: What cash costs and owner time are attributable to the
    site today?
  - Unlocks: a real profit calculation and a ceiling for automated service or
    agent spend.
  - Resolution evidence: a dated cost ledger with recurring, variable and
    one-off costs separated.

- **Inventory current commercial relationships**
  - Type: research
  - Question or outcome: What are the current terms, clicks, revenue,
    disclosure state, expiry rules and contact owners for Logos, Ticket Tailor
    and Hallmaster?
  - Unlocks: a safe monetisation baseline and stale-affiliate procedure.
  - Resolution evidence: one private register reconciled with every public
    commercial link and available platform statement.

- **Approve the minimum-owner agent protocol**
  - Type: decision
  - Question or outcome: Should the four-tier authority model, 90-minute
    standard packet and 120-minute hard monthly cap become the basis for
    implementation?
  - Recommendation: approve the prototype as the operating-design basis. It
    gives agents wide private preparation authority, preserves human approval
    for external and consequential work, and makes non-response safely pause
    rather than publish work.
  - Unlocks: implementation controls, a real monthly operating trial,
    measurable owner time and the maintenance-model milestone.
  - Resolution evidence: Bryn approves
    `docs/AGENT_OPERATING_PROTOCOL_PROTOTYPE.md` as written or names the
    authority or time-budget rules that must change.

### Blocked

- **Record qualified-demand baseline**
  - Blocked by: access to GA4, Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and any
    affiliate dashboards, plus the agreed measurement window.

- **Choose the first commercial experiment**
  - Blocked by: qualified-demand baseline, current affiliate economics and a
    short consultation with likely church buyers or partners.

- **Select the next acquisition asset**
  - Blocked by: actual search and decision-tool behaviour; current plans
    explicitly gate new high-intent pages on that evidence.

- **Automate recurring site operation**
  - Blocked by: authority tiers, auditable workflows, safe credentials,
    rollback, incident handling and proof that automation saves more time than
    it creates.

## Fog

- Whether the highest-value buyer is an individual church, a diocese/network,
  a supplier, an implementation partner or a mixed model.
- Whether a church-paid procurement resource, disclosed affiliate conversion,
  qualified implementation referral, sponsorship or another format has the
  best combination of demand, margin, low maintenance and reputation fit.
- The traffic and conversion volume needed for the first viable model; this
  cannot be calculated until commission or price, conversion and maintenance
  evidence exist.
- Whether public association with Bryn should become more visible to build
  trust or remain deliberately limited to reduce personal reputation exposure.
- Which external systems should run recurring agent work. Tooling should follow
  the approved operating protocol, not determine it.
- Whether the catalogue should remain at 147 profiles once true maintenance
  cost and qualified-use distribution are known.

## Decisions and findings

- **Minimum-owner agent protocol prototype**
  - Result: a provisional four-tier protocol now assigns recurring work to
    autonomous monitoring, autonomous preparation, batch-approved execution or
    stop-and-escalate authority. Its standard monthly packet allows up to five
    decisions and two Bryn send-or-post actions, plans 90 minutes and enforces a
    120-minute hard cap.
  - Basis: `docs/AGENT_OPERATING_PROTOCOL_PROTOTYPE.md`, the existing agent and
    human boundaries in `docs/GROWTH_STRATEGY.md`, the maintenance workflows
    and the agreed arrival conditions.
  - Implication: the design question is concrete enough for Bryn to approve or
    amend. The document grants no operating authority until that decision and
    separate implementation work occur.
  - Date: 31 July 2026

- **Operating target and reputation budget**
  - Result: arrival requires £500 monthly operating profit for three
    consecutive months, no more than two planned hours of Bryn's time per month,
    and six months without an undisclosed commercial relationship, fabricated
    claim, unauthorised external message or unresolved high-severity factual
    incident.
  - Basis: Bryn's decision. A separate wayfinder will cover further growth once
    the £500 target is reached.
  - Implication: the destination is now testable, this map is active and the
    agent authority model, profit ledger and stop conditions can be designed
    against fixed thresholds.
  - Date: 31 July 2026

- **Trust is a production constraint**
  - Result: reputation protection is expressed as authority limits, detection,
    disclosure, correction and stop rules rather than an impossible guarantee.
  - Basis: the project's principles, growth strategy, public methodology and
    current UK commercial-disclosure guidance.
  - Implication: full autonomous publishing and outreach are not part of the
    route; exception-based human accountability is.
  - Date: 31 July 2026

- **Profit must include owner time**
  - Result: profitability will not be measured as affiliate receipts minus
    hosting alone.
  - Basis: the destination explicitly requires little Bryn input; excluding
    that resource would hide the principal constraint.
  - Implication: time tracking is part of the baseline and commercial gates.
  - Date: 31 July 2026

- **Demand precedes expansion**
  - Result: the next growth asset and commercial model remain gated on actual
    search, decision-use and referral data.
  - Basis: the existing roadmap and growth strategy already require this and
    the repository contains no performance baseline.
  - Implication: do not add a broad listing or guide batch to simulate growth.
  - Date: 31 July 2026

## Out of scope

- A guarantee that no person will ever criticise the site or associate it
  negatively with Bryn.
- Mass AI-generated listings, guides, reviews, rankings, outreach or social
  posts.
- Automatic negotiation, supplier dispute handling, legal response or
  relationship-based messaging in Bryn's name.
- Paid ranking, hidden affiliate treatment, editorial approval rights for
  sponsors or suppression of legitimate caveats.
- A large CMS, CRM, database, sales team or multi-channel media operation before
  a validated workflow requires it.
- Detailed dates and revenue forecasts for milestones whose demand, price and
  conversion dependencies remain unresolved.
