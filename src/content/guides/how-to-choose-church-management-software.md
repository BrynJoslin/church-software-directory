Buying a church management system is not mainly a feature-matching exercise. It is a decision about how people, responsibilities and sensitive information will be organised.

## 1. Start with the problem

Write down the tasks that are currently unreliable, duplicated or too time-consuming. Name who experiences each problem and what a better outcome would look like. If the only requirement is “one place for everything”, the work is not specific enough yet.

## 2. Map the information

List the personal data, giving records, pastoral notes, children's information and volunteer details involved. Decide which roles genuinely need access. Ask suppliers how permissions, audit history, retention and exports work, then assess those answers against your church's own responsibilities.

## 3. Test real workflows

Use a trial with representative, non-sensitive sample data. Ask the people who will do the work to test common tasks: adding a newcomer, changing an address, building a rota, recording attendance and exporting a report.

## 4. Calculate the complete cost

Include subscriptions, modules, contacts, messages, payment fees, onboarding, training, migration and staff time. Check how the price changes as the church grows.

## 5. Plan the exit before the entry

Confirm what can be exported, in which formats, and what happens at cancellation. A responsible choice includes a credible route out.

## A useful shortlist

Compare two to four plausible options using confirmed facts. Record unknowns as questions for the supplier rather than scoring them as absent features.
