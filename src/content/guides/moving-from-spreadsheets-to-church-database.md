## Quick answer

Move from spreadsheets to a church database when the same people data is being copied between lists, important tasks depend on one person remembering a workaround, or different authorised people need different views of the same current record. A database is not automatically safer or more useful: it adds permissions, training, data-quality and ownership work. For a small church with one well-owned list and a few repeatable tasks, a documented spreadsheet process may remain the better option.

The responsible route is to improve the records before importing them. Identify the purpose of every field, remove duplicate and unnecessary copies, agree who owns each workflow, map a small set of records, test it with minimised data and reconcile the result before turning the old process off. This guide was researched on 28 July 2026 for UK churches. It is not legal, safeguarding or data-protection advice.

## Decide whether a database is the real answer

The issue is rarely that a spreadsheet is old. The issue is that it no longer gives the church a dependable shared process. A list can be adequate for a single purpose: a small rota, a simple attendance count or a tightly controlled contact list. It becomes fragile when it has to act as a people database, a communication register, an event system, a record of pastoral actions and a reporting tool at the same time.

Write down one failure that matters. For example: “Three people maintain contact details in separate files, and a welcome-team follow-up is missed when one file is not updated.” That statement gives the project a testable aim. “We need a modern database” does not.

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) explains that personal data should be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose in its [data-minimisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/data-minimisation/). That is a reason to reduce uncontrolled copies; it is not a reason to import every historic column into a new system.

Stay with a spreadsheet, at least for now, where all of these are true:

- one named person keeps the master list and a second authorised person can take it over;
- the fields have a clear purpose and permissions are proportionate;
- the church can find, correct, export and securely back up the information it needs; and
- communications, attendance, giving, safeguarding and finance are not being improvised in the same file.

Consider a database where records are repeatedly duplicated, several ministries need different authorised access, event or group processes are hard to trace, or reporting requires manual reconciliation. Software cannot settle whether a person should be contacted, whether a pastoral note should exist or who is accountable for a safeguarding concern. Those are governance and judgement questions first.

## Set a boundary before comparing products

This guide is for churches moving routine people, group, event, rota or communications records from spreadsheets into a shared church database. It excludes specialist safeguarding casework, charity accounting ledgers, Gift Aid claims and formal archival systems. Those may connect to a database, but they need their own evidence, permissions and professional or denominational advice.

Treat the examples below as representative directory listings, not a market ranking. They were selected because their directory records describe broad church-management workflows and publish source links. They were not hands-on tested for this guide. Supplier material establishes what a supplier says it provides; it does not prove that a church's data, access model or governance is appropriate.

Give the project four named roles. A decision sponsor agrees the scope and budget. A day-to-day owner maintains data standards after launch. Operational testers represent the people who enter and use information. A data-and-governance reviewer checks that sensitive fields, access and retention questions are not being silently carried forward. In a small church, one volunteer may hold several roles, but each responsibility should still be visible.

For Church of England bodies, the [Church of England records and information management guidance](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/libraries-and-archives/records-and-information-management) points to its May 2026 Record Retention Schedule and a record-keeping review process. Other churches should use the relevant national, denominational and legal context rather than assuming that a parish schedule applies unchanged.

## Inventory, minimise and map the records

Do not start by exporting the largest spreadsheet. Start with a data inventory. List each file, its owner, the people it covers, its purpose, the fields it contains, who can access it, how often it changes and what happens if it is unavailable. Include informal copies in email attachments, personal cloud drives, volunteer devices and contact lists. The aim is not to accuse people of poor practice; it is to discover the real system before changing it.

Use this migration rehearsal with fictional or minimised records:

1. Choose one workflow, such as a new person's welcome journey or a small-group list.
2. Define the minimum fields needed for that workflow and the role allowed to see each one.
3. Mark fields as import, archive separately, delete under the church's applicable policy, or investigate.
4. Match each import field to one destination field and write a plain-language reason for the mapping.
5. Import a small test set, including a duplicate, a missing email address and a record that should have restricted access.
6. Ask two operational testers to complete the normal task without referring to the old spreadsheet.
7. Compare the imported records, permissions and outputs with the agreed mapping, then record defects before any wider import.

The output should be a short acceptance checklist, not an assertion that the transfer is complete. The Church of England guidance recommends a records review over time; it does not authorise deletion without checking the applicable retention and archival position. Keep a clear record of who approved the mapping and any decision to retain, archive or remove data.

| Check | Evidence to retain | Completion gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | One sentence for each imported field | A reviewer can explain why the field is needed. |
| Quality | Duplicate, missing-value and format checks | Test records match the approved source or are logged as exceptions. |
| Access | Role list and screenshots or supplier evidence | Testers cannot see fields outside their role. |
| Workflow | A short end-to-end test script | Two users can complete the task without the old file. |
| Exit | Export sample and archive plan | The church can recover usable records if the project stops. |

## Compare the operating model, not the feature list

For this decision, assess how the product will hold the church's agreed record, not whether it advertises the largest set of modules. Ask the same questions of each candidate: can it support the priority workflow; can different roles access only what they need; can the church import and later export usable records; can an ordinary volunteer be trained; and what becomes the authoritative source when the system connects to a form, giving tool or email platform?

[ChurchSuite](/software/churchsuite/) is relevant where a church wants connected people, groups, rotas, events, communications and giving workflows in one system. Its directory record includes published UK pricing and source-backed details about permissions, exports and hosting. It may fit a church that can name an administrator and agree shared working practices. First check: import a minimised welcome-and-group workflow and test access for a volunteer, ministry lead and administrator. Settle before buying: whether the church's sensitive-record boundaries, full export needs and total contact-band cost are documented for its use.

[iKnow Church](/software/iknow-church/) is another church-management option worth exploring where people records, rotas, events and communications are the immediate decision. Its listing should be read for its recorded sources and specific questions rather than as a direct equivalence claim. First check: ask the supplier to demonstrate the exact import, permission, routine update and export journey the church has mapped. Settle before buying: contract, data-processing, retention and exit arrangements that are material to the church's records.

A specialist tool or a clearer shared process can still be the answer. Do not consolidate every spreadsheet merely because an all-in-one system exists. The value of one source of truth depends on agreeing what that truth is, who maintains it and when another system remains authoritative.

## Implement, reconcile and hand over

Plan a controlled cut-over rather than a single import day. Freeze only the records needed for the pilot, keep the old source readable, and decide how changes made during parallel running will be reconciled. Never ask volunteers to update two systems indefinitely: set a short, named period and make one person responsible for resolving differences.

Before a live import, test the things that usually fail quietly: names with punctuation, household relationships, duplicate email addresses, people without email, withdrawn communication permissions, former members, and restricted notes that should not move into a routine database. Do not put safeguarding case information into a general people record merely because a custom field exists.

At handover, record the database owner, backup and supplier-contact route, access-request process, training materials, review date, export location and the decision about the old spreadsheet. The [ICO's guidance on controller–processor contracts](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/) is relevant when a supplier processes personal data for the church, including arrangements at the end of the contract. Read the supplier's current documentation and obtain advice where the church handles sensitive or complex records.

## Software listings to explore

These listings provide structured evidence and supplier questions; they are not an endorsement or a claim that either product suits every church.

- [ChurchSuite](/software/churchsuite/) for connected people, groups, rotas, events and communications workflows, with a documented UK church context.

Your next step is to run the seven-step migration rehearsal on one low-risk workflow. Do not request a full-data import or sign a contract until the owner, field purposes, access model, test result and exit question are written down.
