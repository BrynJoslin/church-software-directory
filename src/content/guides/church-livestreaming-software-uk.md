## Quick answer

Choose a livestreaming platform or service only after deciding what the church is trying to offer: a public broadcast, an interactive online congregation, a short-lived recording, a lasting archive, or a simpler audio service. A platform is one part of the operating model. Camera positions, consent, music rights, captions, volunteer confidence, internet resilience and archive controls usually matter more than a feature comparison. Start with a small, repeatable service format and a clear fallback if the stream fails. Do not assume that a free public platform removes the church's responsibilities for people, content or records.

This guide is not copyright, safeguarding, data-protection or accessibility advice. Use current denominational, licensing and professional advice before streaming identifiable people, children, music or sensitive services.

## Choose the worship and publication model first

Describe the intended audience and what it needs to do. A public YouTube broadcast can help people watch later, but has a different privacy and interaction model from Zoom or Teams. An interactive service may suit a small online congregation but needs a moderator and clearer participant expectations. A managed installation may help where volunteers cannot run live production; it also creates a supplier, support and contract dependency. Audio-only can be a more accessible and sustainable starting point for some churches.

The Methodist Church's current platform comparison says there is no single right platform: the appropriate choice depends on congregation, budget, volunteer confidence and how the community wants to access worship.[^methodist-platform] Test that assertion against the local church rather than copying another congregation's equipment list.

Keep live production separate from sermon hosting. A service stream may be temporary while selected sermons or teaching belong in a separate, edited archive. Decide whether each output is public, unlisted, member-only or not retained.

## Set consent, safeguarding and camera boundaries

Make it possible to attend worship without appearing on camera. The Methodist consent and accessibility guidance recommends clear notice, an off-camera area, careful camera operation, particular care with children and a documented retention approach.[^methodist-consent] These are practical controls, not a universal consent formula. Follow the policy and advice that applies to the church.

Before a pilot, decide who can be filmed, where cameras may point, how an unexpected person entering shot is handled, and who can pause or end a stream. Obtain appropriate agreement from people who will be clearly identifiable, including preachers, readers, musicians and interviewees. Do not rely on a congregational notice to resolve every identifiable appearance or copyright issue.

Children and adults at risk need specific care. The stream must not become an informal record of attendance, a substitute for an agreed consent process or a reason to delay a safeguarding response. Train camera operators and stewards on the escalation route.

## Treat copyright and accessibility as service design

Do not select a platform first and investigate music rights afterwards. The Methodist copyright guidance says online worship, including livestreams and podcasts, needs attention to permissions and that showing words, reading them aloud or placing them online may require permission unless public-domain status is certain.[^methodist-copyright] PRS for Music similarly states that its Digital Music Licence for Worship is needed for online streaming where relevant; confirm the current scope and licence position for every use.[^prs]

Make accessibility a planned output rather than an optional enhancement. Captions, clear audio, readable service material and an understandable audio-only experience can make a material difference. Test automatic captions with names, hymns and theological words, then correct important recordings where possible. Ask actual online participants what prevents access; an impressive camera cannot compensate for inaudible speech.

| Requirement | Trial question | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consent and privacy | Can a person attend off camera? | Notice, seating plan and operator briefing |
| Copyright | Are every music and spoken-content permissions understood? | Current licence/permission record |
| Captions | Can viewers enable and understand them? | Test recording and correction process |
| Audio | Does speech remain clear on a phone and headphones? | Recorded sound check |
| Resilience | What happens if broadband or a volunteer fails? | Written fallback and contact list |
| Archive | Who removes, edits or retains recordings? | Retention/review schedule |

## Run a low-risk pilot and failure drill

Use a short, planned test rather than a major festival or all-age service. Publish only material the church has prepared to record. Test the complete workflow: operator setup, audio monitoring, captions, moderator route, notice, stream start, unexpected interruption, end and archive handling.

1. Record a private test and listen on several ordinary devices.
2. Check framing against the off-camera boundary and consent plan.
3. Test the copyright and content list before going live.
4. Enable captions and assess their accuracy with a viewer who has not seen the script.
5. Disconnect the normal internet or device and practise the agreed response.
6. Publish, unpublish and remove a test recording to confirm the archive route.
7. Hold a short review with worship leaders, technical volunteers and online participants.

Do not make a pilot permanent by neglect. Agree in advance whether it will be deleted, how feedback is collected and what evidence is required before a regular service starts.

## Implement with realistic ownership and exit

Name a service owner, technical operator, safeguarding/contact escalation person and archive owner. Avoid an arrangement that works only when one volunteer is present. Document login ownership, payment method, equipment inventory, recordings location and a simple handover for a new operator.

Review after several services. Keep only the level of production the church can sustain; a dependable phone or audio workflow can be more inclusive than an elaborate stream that frequently fails. If an external service or platform changes terms, removes a recording or ends a feature, the church should still know where its source files and permissions records are held.

Make the service easy to find without making the church's whole online life dependent on a social platform. Keep a stable page on the church website with the schedule, direct access route, contact alternative and accessibility information. Test that page with keyboard-only use and a mobile connection before directing a congregation to it, including the first-time visitor journey. Keep clear written instructions for substitutes and emergency contacts.
