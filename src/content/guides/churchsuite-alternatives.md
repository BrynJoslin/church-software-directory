## Quick answer

There is no single better alternative to ChurchSuite for a UK church. ChurchSuite combines a people database with operational modules and publishes UK pricing, VAT treatment and a 30-day trial; its Giving documentation also describes a UK Gift Aid declaration workflow. <a href="https://churchsuite.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchSuite pricing</a> and <a href="https://support.churchsuite.com/article/118-managing-your-gift-aid-declarations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchSuite Gift Aid declarations</a>.

Only change where another system solves one named problem much better. The main trade-off is often not a missing feature: it is losing a connected Gift Aid, people, rota and member-access workflow, or taking on the work to rebuild it.

| If your real reason for looking is… | Start with | Do not decide until you have checked… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Better worship planning, rotas or an extensible product set | [Planning Center](/software/planning-center/) | The full module cost, UK giving and Gift Aid process, and data hand-offs between products. |
| Parish or UK-focused administration and giving | [iKnow Church](/software/iknow-church/) | The exact Gift Aid, payment, migration and permissions workflow for your church. |
| A lower-cost way to evaluate a broad system | [ChMeetings](/software/chmeetings/) or [ChurchTrac](/software/churchtrac/) | Currency, payment options, Gift Aid, data processing and the cost after the trial or free tier. |
| European hosting information and an API-led platform | [ChurchTools](/software/churchtools/) | The contract, export route, UK finance workflow and technical administration needed. |
| Frustration with the current setup rather than a proven product gap | Staying with ChurchSuite for now | Whether training, permissions, ownership or data quality would solve the problem without migration. |

This guide was researched on 26 and 27 July 2026. It is a representative shortlist, not a complete market survey or a claim of hands-on testing.

## Who this guide is for and how the options were selected

This guide is for UK church administrators, operations staff, ministry leaders, treasurers and trustees considering a replacement for ChurchSuite. It covers broad church-management systems that can take on a meaningful share of the people, operations, giving or service-planning work. It does not choose specialist accounting, safeguarding case-management, payment or presentation software on its own.

The options were selected before drafting because they represent materially different operating models: a modular service-planning ecosystem, UK-focused administration, low-cost evaluation, a broad all-in-one trial and European-hosted technical flexibility. They are not ranked and inclusion does not imply sponsorship, affiliation or endorsement.

The product information comes primarily from supplier pricing, product and support documentation. The supplied research also reviewed independent-review listings and public church-technology discussions. Those sources can indicate themes or questions, but their samples, recency and incentives vary, so this guide does not convert them into ratings or claims about general user experience. It does not report supplier interviews, church trials or original user research.

## Changing system means changing how work runs

“ChurchSuite alternatives” sounds like a product-comparison query. For most churches, it is a migration decision. The relevant question is therefore: **which operational constraint is serious enough that a different operating model is worth the disruption?**

ChurchSuite's UK pricing is contact- and module-based. Its published plans range from a single module to a complete suite, and its support material describes the Address Book as the foundation of the system. <a href="https://churchsuite.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchSuite's pricing page</a> also says active Address Book contacts drive billing and that UK customers can pay by monthly Direct Debit. That does not make it right for every church, but it explains why a headline subscription comparison can miss the real cost of replacing connected work.

For example, a church may be dissatisfied with rotas. Planning Center may be a stronger fit if service preparation and volunteer scheduling are genuinely the central constraint. But if the church also relies on a connected UK Gift Aid process, the move creates a finance design task rather than simply swapping one rota screen for another. The same applies to a cheaper or free plan: it may reduce subscription cost while increasing the burden of payment, data, support or integration decisions.

Do not switch to solve a problem that is actually one of these:

- duplicate or incomplete records that no one owns;
- an unclear policy for access to pastoral, children’s or giving data;
- volunteers who have not been trained on the current workflow;
- a process with no named leader; or
- an expectation that one system will remove the need for financial controls, safeguarding judgement or pastoral discretion.

A shared spreadsheet, a documented rota process, a focused specialist tool or a short ChurchSuite configuration review may be the more responsible next step when the church has a narrow problem. Technology can support a clear process; it cannot create ownership for one.

## Use the same decision criteria for every alternative

Before arranging a demonstration, write one sentence describing the change you need. For example: “Our worship leaders need to prepare services and manage availability without copying details between three tools.” That sentence prevents an impressive feature list from deciding the shortlist.

Use these criteria for every candidate:

| Criterion | What to establish | Why it changes the UK decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core workflow | Can ordinary staff and volunteers complete the actual people, rota, event or reporting task? | A system that adds work at the weekly volunteer hand-off is not an improvement. |
| Giving and Gift Aid | If giving is in scope, can the supplier demonstrate declarations, eligible gifts, claims or exports, corrections and reconciliation? | A generic donation feature does not prove that the church can run its required UK process. |
| Total first-year cost | Record subscription, VAT, currency conversion, payment fees, messaging, migration, training and any connected products. | A low entry price can hide the cost of a full replacement. |
| Data and permissions | Ask about roles, exports, audit information, processor terms, sub-processors and end-of-contract arrangements. | The church remains responsible for its own data governance. The ICO identifies controller–processor contracts and the end of the contract as material areas to address. <a href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ICO controller–processor guidance</a>. |
| Operating burden | Name the administrator, training plan, supplier support route and any technical owner. | A product can be affordable to buy but unsustainable to operate. |
| Migration and exit | Test import quality and a usable export; document what will be archived, retained or deleted. | The church needs a route out before it gives a new supplier live information. |

Missing evidence is not a negative score. It is a supplier question. Make the answer part of the written decision record rather than treating an unanswered question as a feature gap or a reassurance.

## Alternatives grouped by the trade-off they make

### Planning Center: a modular service-planning ecosystem

[Planning Center](/software/planning-center/) is in scope for churches whose principal reason for leaving is worship planning, scheduling, check-in, registrations or a more modular product ecosystem. Its pricing page lists People as a free member database and charges separately for other products, with free capacity-limited tiers and a 30-day initial period. <a href="https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Planning Center pricing</a>.

That model can be a strength: a church can use only the products it needs. The trade-off is that a ChurchSuite-equivalent setup must be costed as a combination of products and usage limits, not compared with one starting price. Its public pricing is in US dollars. The supplied research did not establish a native UK Gift Aid claim workflow, so a church that needs one should require a demonstration of its exact giving, declaration, finance-export and reconciliation process.

**First practical check:** run one full service cycle: create a service, invite a team, record unavailability, communicate a change, run check-in or registration if needed, and export the result. Separately ask the treasurer how the proposed giving process would work in the UK.

### iKnow Church: UK administration and Gift Aid evidence

[iKnow Church](/software/iknow-church/) is in scope where parish administration, pound-sterling pricing or Gift Aid workflow are central to the decision. The supplier publishes VAT-inclusive prices based on adult members and describes HMRC Gift Aid claim submission in its donation material. <a href="https://www.iknowchurch.co.uk/iknow" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">iKnow Church pricing</a> and <a href="https://www.iknowchurch.co.uk/charges/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">iKnow Church finance charges</a>.

This is stronger evidence of UK-oriented administration than a generic claim that a product is available to British churches. It is not a conclusion about suitability or compliance. The supplied research found limited independent evidence and some public comments about an older product experience; neither should substitute for a current demonstration.

**First practical check:** have the Gift Aid secretary or treasurer test a declaration, donation import, claim or export, correction, report and accounting hand-off. Then test the permissions a parish administrator and ministry volunteer would actually receive.

### ChMeetings and ChurchTrac: lower-cost evaluation routes

[ChMeetings](/software/chmeetings/) provides a free plan for up to 50 people and publishes paid plans from US$12 a month for up to 100 people; it also advertises a 30-day trial. <a href="https://www.chmeetings.com/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChMeetings pricing</a>. Its UK marketing describes Gift Aid and UK church administration, but supplier marketing alone does not show the depth of the declaration-to-claim workflow your church needs.

[ChurchTrac](/software/churchtrac/) is a broader all-in-one option with people, giving, check-in, groups, planning and optional accounting or messaging. It publishes a 30-day trial and a tracked-name pricing model. <a href="https://www.churchtrac.com/pricing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchTrac pricing</a>. The directory's current source record does not establish a UK Gift Aid workflow, pounds-sterling payment arrangement or data-processing position; those are questions to settle, not reasons to rule it out.

Both are sensible candidates when the church needs a low-risk evaluation of a broad alternative. Their common trade-off is that the first visible price is not evidence of UK finance, payment, support or data fit.

**First practical check:** use a fictional or minimised dataset to test people records, permissions, a rota, a report and a data export. If the church collects donations, ask the supplier to show the full UK process before any live finance data is imported.

### ChurchTools: European hosting information and technical flexibility

[ChurchTools](/software/churchtools/) is in scope for a church that values supplier-published German hosting, service planning and an open API. The supplier publishes person-based packages in euros, a 30-day trial and migration support as a paid service. <a href="https://church.tools/en/pricing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchTools pricing</a> and <a href="https://church.tools/en/data-security/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ChurchTools data security information</a>.

Hosting geography is relevant evidence, not a compliance certificate. A UK church still needs to review the current data-processing terms, sub-processors, permissions, retention, access and export route. The supplied research did not find public documentation of a UK Gift Aid workflow, so it should not be inferred from broad platform capability.

**First practical check:** ask the person who would own administration to build a people-record workflow, a rota and an export. Ask the treasurer how Gift Aid, payments and finance hand-off would be handled; ask the supplier for the current processor and exit terms in writing.

## A fair trial and migration decision record

Give every shortlisted option the same test. Use fictional or minimised data until the church has approved its data-sharing basis and supplier terms.

1. Import a small contact file, identify a duplicate and correct it.
2. Give a ministry leader the intended role and check what they can see, edit and export.
3. Create a rota, record unavailability, publish a change and fill a gap.
4. Create an event or group, capture attendance and produce the report a leader needs.
5. Where giving matters, test the exact declaration, donation, claim or export, correction and reconciliation workflow.
6. Export the test records and open them in a normal spreadsheet without supplier assistance.

Record the result in a one-page decision record:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Problem we are solving | One clear operational problem, not a feature wish. |
| Candidate and exact plan | Product, modules, usage band, currency, VAT and term. |
| Workflow result | Pass, workaround or unresolved question; name the tester. |
| UK finance and data evidence | Link or supplier answer for Gift Aid, payment, processor terms and export. |
| First-year cost | Subscription plus migration, training, fees, messages and connected products. |
| Owner and review date | Named day-to-day owner and post-launch access/data review date. |
| Exit plan | Export tested, records to archive and cancellation terms checked. |

If no candidate produces a material improvement in the chosen workflow, do not migrate. Address the current process, training or ownership problem and review the decision later.

## Implementation, migration and next step

Switching systems is a change to church operations, not a data-upload exercise. Name a migration owner and make an inventory of people and household records, consent information, groups, attendance, rotas, forms, documents, giving data and reports. Decide what should move, what should be retained under the church’s own retention arrangements and what should not enter the new system.

Before committing, test an export from ChurchSuite and the proposed system, agree who may approve access, and make a training plan for staff and volunteers. Do not assume that a supplier’s security or data-protection material transfers the church’s responsibility. The church should also schedule an access and data-quality review about three months after launch.

Your next step is simple: write the single operational problem prompting the search, choose no more than two candidates from the table above, and run the same six-task trial with the people who will own the work. Keep ChurchSuite if neither candidate produces a demonstrable, worthwhile improvement.
