## Quick answer

Choose children’s check-in software when a church needs a dependable way to register children, know their group and return them to the right parent or carer. It should support a carefully designed local procedure; it does not create safeguarding practice, decide who is authorised, replace safer recruitment or resolve an incident. Start with the handover the church wants to run, then test it with real volunteers and only the information they need. Include a failure drill for a missing badge, visitor, delayed collection, device or internet outage.

Keep check-in distinct from safeguarding casework. A check-in record may be operational evidence of attendance, but it is not an incident-management or safeguarding-decision system. If a concern arises, follow the church's current safeguarding route rather than relying on a note or flag in the check-in app.

## Map the family journey and handover

Draw the journey for a regular family, a visiting family and a child arriving without the expected adult. Include arrival, registration, group assignment, badge or collection reference, changes during the session, collection and any uncollected child. Decide who can authorise a change to the normal pickup arrangement and how the team confirms it. Do not make a system's default badge, QR code or contact field the whole rule.

Ask the children's lead, check-in volunteer, group leader and safeguarding lead to walk it together. Record what information must be visible at the point of care, what must stay limited to a smaller group, and what should never be placed in a routine check-in note. The aim is a calm, repeatable welcome that still works for a new volunteer and an anxious parent.

## Collect only the information the session needs

Children's registration can include contact details, allergies, health information, additional needs, photos and consent choices. Some of that may be sensitive. The ICO's children's guidance says organisations should collect and retain only the minimum personal data needed for the service being provided.[^ico-children] Set each field against a purpose: emergency contact, attendance, dietary preparation, accessibility arrangement or communication preference. If no one can name the purpose and access need, leave it out.

The ChurchSuite Check-In support documentation illustrates the sort of operational information a product may show to authorised leaders, including emergency contacts and pertinent allergy or medical information.[^churchsuite] That describes one supplier's published feature set; it does not establish which fields a particular church should collect or who should see them.

Agree who may add or amend a child's record, who may see medical or allergy information, who may print labels, who can run attendance reports and who administers user accounts. Test these roles with least-privilege accounts. A user who can check a child in should not automatically be able to browse every family record or export a ministry database.

## Compare the operational controls

Use a structured trial. Ask suppliers to show the following rather than presenting a generic dashboard.

| Decision area | Demonstration to request | Question to settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular check-in | Register a linked child and adult | Is the normal route quick without exposing extra data? |
| Visiting family | Add a visitor with minimal details | Can the church avoid creating an over-detailed record? |
| Collection | Match child and adult collection evidence | What happens when the normal collector changes? |
| Group capacity | Move a child to another group | Can leaders see the information they need without broad access? |
| Permission | Use a volunteer-level account | Which actions and exports remain possible? |
| Continuity | Turn off the device or connection | What paper or manual fallback is agreed? |

Labels and collection codes can help organise a handover, but the church must decide how staff respond to a mismatch or uncertainty. Do not promise parents that a code alone makes a collection safe. Explain the human escalation path and train it.

## Run a fictional family and failure drill

Do not use real child data in a new trial. Create fictional families and run these tests in the actual welcome area.

1. Check in a regular child and confirm the group, parent/carer reference and authorised leader view.
2. Add a visiting child with only the agreed minimum information and test later removal or review.
3. Change a pickup arrangement through the church's approved human procedure, not by an unverified verbal request.
4. Simulate an allergy or additional-needs prompt and confirm that only the right team sees it.
5. Misplace a collection badge or code and practise the escalation and identity-check procedure.
6. Turn off the usual device, printer or internet connection and use the written fallback register.
7. Reconcile attendance and check that the record is accessible only to appropriate roles afterwards.

The trial should expose queues, unclear instructions and volunteer training gaps. A faster kiosk is not an improvement if it makes visitor handling or collection ambiguous. Test accessibility too: height, lighting, language, parent assistance and the ability to use the process without a smartphone.

## Implement alongside policy, training and review

Document the final procedure in plain language beside the check-in point. Include who opens the session, where spare labels or paper registers are held, when leaders receive the children, who responds to a collection concern and how an incident is recorded outside the routine attendance system. Train every role before the first live session and refresh it when the product or procedure changes.

Review access after volunteer or staff changes. The Charity Commission's safeguarding guidance expects charities to have appropriate policies, procedures, checks and reporting arrangements; software can support administration but cannot demonstrate that those arrangements are in place.[^charity]

At the first post-launch review, ask parents and volunteers where the process felt unclear, slow or intrusive. Correct the procedure, signage and training before adding optional features or collecting more information. An ordinary, well-rehearsed handover is more valuable than an unused feature that makes the register harder to understand.

Decide how long routine attendance and visitor details are retained under the church's applicable records approach. A check-in platform's retention options are implementation controls, not the church's retention policy. For Church of England bodies, the current records resource points to its 2026 retention schedule and record-keeping review guidance.[^cofe-records]
